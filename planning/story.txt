The only possible explanation for Halli and Pafu ascending to the heavens after a Game Over is that they are guardian beings supposedly sent to keep nature in balance. Xaraxos gets to be one as well - that's why he can use magic. Just like Tolkien's Istari, none of them actually bother to follow through with the mission and were probably never even told what it was in the first place. So that's where the setting begins.

In truth, the heavens really don't pay attention to what's happening to all the lowlifes down there, because society is being built at a steady pace by the Beavercrat League. So the heavens' guardian beings can really just stuff around with very few consequences if they want. And that's basically what they do.

Beavercrats are internally polarised between the fiberite and candevan factions. Any beaver will insist they couldn't be more different to each other, but they're pretty indistinguishable to the outside observer. They all hate the pelican repellers, but candevans are a bit more pragmatic about it.  

Narration drives the story, but "Xaraxos" hijacks the narration at some point to mess with the player.

Narrative-driven video games often use the protagonist to represent the player's inability to control the story, but I want my game to say something different about the player.
- RDR2: Morgan is suspiciously skilled but unambitious; a useful tool for Dutch to throw missions at. Lacks purpose in life despite pressing buttons on a controller to get dopamine.
- RDR1: Marston Jr is suspiciously skilled but depressed existing in a world where he doesn't belong. Lacks purpose in life despite pressing buttons on a controller to get dopamine.
- Uncharted 4: Drake is suspiciously skilled but bored living in a world without awesome stuff to do. Lacks purpose in life until he gets to press buttons on a controller to get dopamine.
- Spec Ops: The Line: Walker's desperate need for validation seals his fate, and he and the player are unable to accept one thing causes the other.
- TLOU2: No matter how powerful Ellie gets, it doesn't seem to help the fact that Joel's dead.
- Metal Gear V: Snake wants to be Snake, but it turns out that he isn't Snake, and he never was Snake.
- Rockman 4 Minus Infinity: Rock is tired of the tedious repetition of Wily's schemes, but is still genuinely surprised by the additional lengths and slight variations that Wily has gone out of his way to provide this time.
